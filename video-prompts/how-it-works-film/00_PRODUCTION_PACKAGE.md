Every guess is marked **[ASSUMPTION]**. Correct anything, and the crew changes only what you name.

## What changed in v5: Veo speaks the voice inside every clip
- **Every clip prompt now opens with the same locked narrator block** (word for word in all 8):
  > VOICE: this video MUST include a spoken English voiceover by one off-screen narrator, a calm, warm Indian-English woman in her early 30s, studio quality, speaking slowly and clearly. No one on screen speaks.
- **Then, after the timed shots, a quoted narration line:** `VOICEOVER [00:00-00:08]: the off-screen narrator says warmly, "..."`. Quotation marks are what make Veo speak the words. Each line also gives the delivery (softly, seriously, warmly) and, where needed, how to say "VitaGraph" (VY-tuh-graf).
- **Turn Veo's audio ON.** The tails now say "No music" (we add music in the edit) instead of "No dialogue".
- **Risk, said plainly:** Veo has no saved voice, so each clip is a new performance, and the narrator may sound a little different between clips. The fix is the word-for-word voice block. If two clips still differ:
  - run all the clips through **ElevenLabs Voice Changer** with the "VitaGraph Narrator" voice; or
  - use the backup lines in `09_voiceover_elevenlabs.txt`.
- **Prompt length is now 186–247 words.** The voice needs about 60 more words per clip. Long prompts can make Veo drop instructions, so if a clip ignores a beat, shorten that beat's wording first.
- **Subtitles:** `10_subtitles.srt` is timed to the planned voice. Veo's own speech will start and stop slightly differently, so nudge each subtitle to the real audio in the edit (the words stay the same).

## What changed in v4
- **A new picture every 2–3 seconds:** 23 visual beats in 60 s.
  - Each 8 s clip now holds 3 timed shots, written in Veo 3.1's **timestamp prompting** syntax (`[00:00-00:03] ...`), which is Google's documented multi-shot format.
  - Each beat keeps the crew rules: one shot type, one angle, **one** camera move and one action.
  - A script checked that no two beats in a row (including across clip joins) share both shot type and angle.
- **Tradeoff, said plainly:** three shots need three camera instructions, so the prompts are now **185 words or fewer** (they were 150). The faces and places are written once per clip ("Cast and setting: ..."), not once per beat, which is the layout of Google's own timestamp example.
  - Risk: with more cuts inside a clip, faces can drift. The fix is still the reference stills.
  - If one beat fails, rewrite **only that beat's line**.
- **The voice prompt is now complete** (`09_voiceover_elevenlabs.txt`), in ElevenLabs' official Voice Design format:
  - a voice description, matching preview text, Eleven v3 settings and the 8 tagged lines;
  - pronunciation checks for "VitaGraph" and "AI".
- **Every clip file shows the voice line it carries,** so the picture beats can be matched to the words (show the thing at the moment the voice says it).

## 1. Story, built from four researched frameworks
- **StoryBrand (Donald Miller):** the customer is the hero and the brand is the guide.
  - Hero: **Meena**.
  - Problem: on three levels (below).
  - Guide: **VitaGraph**, with empathy (clips 1–3 show we understand) and authority (clips 4–6 show it works).
  - Plan: **3 steps the viewer can repeat**: it reads (4), it connects (5), you ask and check (6).
  - Failure: data leaving the hospital (3).
  - Success: relief (7), and data that stays home (8).
  - Call to action: "Upload your first report."
- **The problem, on three levels:**
  - External: twenty years of paper.
  - Internal: the doctor's frustration and the patient's worry.
  - Philosophical: private health data should never have to leave the hospital to be useful.
- **Story Spine** (Kenn Adams; often linked to Pixar):

| Beat | Line |
|---|---|
| Once upon a time | Meena kept every report (1). |
| Every day | Her doctor searches the pile (2). |
| But | Most AI sends the data away (3). |
| Because of that | VitaGraph reads every page (4), so it connects every result (5), so you can ask and check (6). |
| Until finally | Relief (7). |
| Ever since then | The data stays home (8). |

- **One idea per scene:** each clip file starts with "THE VIEWER MUST UNDERSTAND", one sentence. If a shot doesn't deliver its sentence, it fails, however beautiful it is.

**Logline:** A grandmother's twenty years of health reports bury her doctor in paper, until VitaGraph turns the pile into answers anyone can check, and every word stays inside the hospital.

## 6. Subtitles (in `10_subtitles.srt`, checked against Netflix's English subtitle rules)
- 42 characters or fewer per line, 2 lines or fewer.
- Reading speed 17 characters per second or less (the highest is 15.9).
- Each subtitle stays up between 5/6 s and 7 s.
- The words match the voice exactly.
- Font in the edit: Archivo 600, white with a soft shadow, bottom centre, inside the bottom 10 % of the frame.

## 7. Editing grammar (what the editor follows)
- **Cut on action** (1→2): the movement carries the eye, so the cut disappears.
- **J-cut** (2→3): the thunder arrives before the picture, a warning.
- **Match cut** (3→4): upward motion out, then upward motion in.
- **Cut mid-motion** (4→5, 5→6), never on a still frame.
- **Cut on the sound** (6→7): the chime lands, then her face.
- **L-cut** (7→8): the room's warmth carries over the outside night.
- **180-degree rule and eyeline match:** see section 3.

## 8. Music and sound
- **The order of priority is voice, then native sound effects, then music.**
- The voice sits at 0 dB.
- The clips' native audio sits around −12 dB. Remove any speech or music Veo added.
- The music bed sits around −20 dB, so it never fights the voice.
- **Music:**
  - 0:00–0:24: solo piano in D minor, sparse.
  - At the match cut (0:24): strings enter and the music turns to D major.
  - 0:40–0:48: a gentle swell.
  - 0:48–0:52: almost silent, so the reaction lands.
  - 0:52–1:00: warm full chord, resolving at 0:58.

## 9. Assembly
1. Lay the 8 voice files at their times.
2. Lay the clips in order, trimming each to its window. Clip 7 is the 4-second clip.
3. Apply the transitions in section 7.
4. Import `10_subtitles.srt`.
5. Grade with the colour script in section 2.
6. Add the end card, 0:56–1:00, over clip 8's empty upper third:
   - "VitaGraph" in Archivo 800, white, with a red square mark;
   - under it, "Answers you can check.";

7. Fade to black over 0:59.5–1:00.
